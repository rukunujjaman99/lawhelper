Family Law

Family matters are heard in the Family Court. Most start with a notice or mediation attempt before a suit is filed.

  1. 1Collect your marriage certificate (kabinnama) and children's birth certificates
  2. 2Talk to a family lawyer about mediation and interim maintenance
  3. 3Your lawyer files in the Family Court and shares the first hearing date

Criminal Law

Cheque dishonour is time-sensitive. A legal notice must go out within the period the law allows after the bank returns the cheque.

  1. 1Keep the cheque and the bank return memo
  2. 2Send a legal notice through your lawyer straight away
  3. 3File the complaint in the Magistrate Court if payment is not made

Property Law

Land cases depend on the chain of title. Lawyers first check the deed, mutation (namjari) and latest khatian records.

  1. 1Gather deeds, khatian and rent receipts
  2. 2Your lawyer verifies the chain of title at the sub-registry
  3. 3A suit for declaration or possession is filed in the Civil Court

Employment Law

The Bangladesh Labour Act sets notice and benefit rules. Complaints go to the Labour Court and have time limits.

  1. 1Ask for the termination letter in writing
  2. 2Keep your appointment letter and salary records
  3. 3Your lawyer sends a notice and files in the Labour Court if needed

Cyber Law

Evidence disappears quickly online, so preserving it early matters most. Complaints go through the cyber-crime process.

  1. 1Take dated screenshots showing the profile link
  2. 2Do not delete messages or reply to the offender
  3. 3Your lawyer files the complaint at the right tribunal

Tax Law

Tax notices carry deadlines. A tax lawyer reviews the assessment and files an appeal before the appellate tribunal if needed.

  1. 1Keep the notice and your returns and invoices
  2. 2Reply within the deadline stated in the notice
  3. 3Your lawyer prepares the appeal and represents you
